# Brand Overview

## What Thruness is

Thruness is a voice-first gait tracking app for equestrians. It listens while
you ride, reads your horse's movement in real time, and builds a picture of the
thing that actually matters: the quality of connection between horse and rider.

## Who it is for

The competitive amateur. The rider who has been in the saddle for years, takes
their training seriously, and wants to understand what is actually happening
underneath them. Not just where they went. How they rode.

Thruness is about more than data. At its heart it is about the bond between
horse and rider — the kind of connection that is felt before it is understood.
Riders know it when it happens. That moment when two living things move as one.
Thruness exists to help them find it more often, understand it more deeply, and
cherish it more completely.

## Positioning statement

> The app that listens while you ride.

Most ride tracking apps measure where you went. Thruness measures how you rode.
It listens while you work, reads your horse's movement in real time, and builds
a picture of the thing that actually matters: the quality of connection between
horse and rider.

## Voice in four words

- **Honest** — We tell riders what their data actually says. We respect them
  enough to give them the truth.
- **Quiet** — We speak when there is something worth saying. We keep it tight.
  We never shout.
- **Knowing** — We understand horses. We speak the language of the arena
  without being exclusive.
- **Reverent** — We treat the bond between horse and rider as something sacred.
  We never reduce it to a metric.

## Who we are not

- Not a yard management app.
- Not a social network.
- Not a fitness tracker with a horse bolted on.
- Not a hardware company.
- Not competing with Equilab on their terms.

We occupy different emotional territory. Less training log, more practice
partner. Less GPS trace, more felt experience. Less performance metric, more
quality of connection.

# Brand Story

## The emotional territory — Calm. Connection. The spiritual bond.

Jen, the founder, describes the relationship between horse and rider as
spiritual. That word is deliberate. Thruness is not a fitness tracker with a
horse bolted on. It is a tool for people who believe that the bond between
horse and rider is something worth paying attention to.

A horse's heart is seven times larger than a human's. That is not just a
biological fact. It is a way of understanding what riders already feel: that
horses are capable of a depth of connection that goes beyond training. Calmness
passes between them. Rhythm is shared. In the best moments, the boundary
between the two disappears.

That is throughness. That is what we are named after. And that is what we help
riders find.

## The mark explained — Two waves. One bond.

The Thruness mark is two waveforms. The thicker line beneath represents the
horse — the foundation. The lighter, responsive line traced above represents
the rider. When they move together, they form a single unified shape.

The mark has three simultaneous readings:

- **The bond** — Two waveforms finding synchronisation. Horse and rider. One
  inside the other.
- **The terrain** — A hillside. The landscape they ride through. The natural
  world the horse belongs to.
- **The canter** — The peak of the mark echoes the asymmetric build-and-float
  of a canter stride. The gait most associated with true connection.

## Who we are not

We are not a yard management app. We are not a social network. We are not a
fitness tracker with a horse bolted on. We are not a hardware company. We are
not competing with Equilab on their terms.

We occupy different emotional territory. Less training log, more practice
partner. Less GPS trace, more felt experience. Less performance metric, more
quality of connection.

## Mark anatomy

The thicker line beneath is the horse — the foundation that carries the ride.
The lighter line traced over the top is the rider, responsive to the movement
underneath. Together they read as terrain. The peak is the moment of
throughness. Three readings, one mark.

## Clear space and minimum size

Maintain a minimum clear space equal to the height of the capital T in the
wordmark. Minimum usage size: 120px wide for the landscape lockup, 40px for
the icon mark alone.

## What not to do

- **Do not stretch or distort.** Always scale proportionally. Never squeeze or
  stretch the mark.
- **Do not recolour the mark.** White, sage deep `#067A60`, or near black
  `#0F1F1A` only.
- **Do not use on clashing backgrounds.** White, warm white, near black, or
  brand gradient only.
- **Do not add effects.** No drop shadows, glows or outlines on the mark
  itself.
- **Do not use wordmark alone at small sizes.** Below 40px use icon mark only.
  The wordmark needs space.
- **Do not place on busy photography.** Ensure sufficient contrast. Use a
  colour overlay if needed.

# Tone of Voice

How we write. What we sound like. The same person, every channel, every time.

## Voice in four words

- **Honest** — We tell riders what their data says. We respect them enough to
  give them the truth.
- **Quiet** — We speak when there is something worth saying. We never shout.
  We never push.
- **Knowing** — We understand horses. We speak the language of the arena
  without being exclusive.
- **Reverent** — We treat the bond between horse and rider as sacred. We never
  reduce it to a metric.

## General voice

**Write like this:**
- Short sentences. Let them land.
- Speak directly to the rider.
- Use concrete, specific language.
- Trust the rider to understand.
- Use the language of equestrian sport naturally.

**Not like this:**
- Long clauses that build to the point.
- Speak about riders in the third person.
- Use vague, motivational filler.
- Over-explain everything.
- Use jargon to sound knowledgeable.

## Words we use and avoid

**Use:**
- Ride, session, work
- Movement, rhythm, connection
- Gait, stride, tempo
- Track, record, analyse
- Horse, rider — by name where possible

**Avoid:**
- Journey, adventure, experience
- Amazing, incredible, awesome
- Leverage, optimise, synergy
- Engage, onboard, action (as a verb)
- Pet, animal, buddy

## App strings and UI labels

Short, verb-led, no punctuation at end of labels. Buttons are actions. Error
messages explain what happened and what to do next.

**Write like this:**
- Start ride
- View session
- Calibrate
- No ride data yet
- Something went wrong. Try again.

**Not like this:**
- Let's get started!
- Click here to view
- Please calibrate your device
- You haven't ridden yet
- Oops! An error occurred.

## Voice prompts (in-app)

The rider is on a horse, hands busy, mind on the work. **Under six words.**
Calm. No questions. **No exclamation marks. Ever.**

**Write like this:**
- Calibration complete.
- Recording.
- Session saved.
- Connection lost. Tap to retry.

**Not like this:**
- Great, we're all set and ready to go!
- I'm listening and recording your ride now!
- Awesome session saved to your profile!
- Uh oh, it looks like we lost connection!

## Writing for the horse

> The bond between horse and rider is spiritual. We write with that weight in
> mind. We never reduce the horse to a data point or the ride to a metric.

**Write like this:**
- Bella's trot showed more regularity this session.
- She's carrying herself better through the corners.
- Something shifted between you both in that last canter.
- He gave everything today.

**Not like this:**
- Your horse's performance score increased.
- The animal's gait data suggests improvement.
- Canter metric improved by 12%.
- Output optimised.

## Quick rules

- **No exclamation marks.** Not in UI, not in push notifications, not in email.
- **Lead with the information.** Don't warm up to the point. Start with it.
- **No emoji in UI.** Emoji in social only, when it adds meaning.
- **UK English always.** Colour, analyse, organise, recognise.
- **Horse = he / she / they.** Never *it*. Never *the animal*. By name where
  possible.
- **Voice prompts under 6 words.** Calm. Verb or noun. No questions.

# UI Components

Core patterns for the app and website. Built on the brand palette and type
system.

## Buttons

All buttons share the same anatomy: full pill shape (`border-radius: 999px`),
PT Sans Bold 14px, 10px × 22px padding, 8px gap to icon, no border for primary,
1–1.5px border for secondary/ghost.

| Variant | Style | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary** | Background `#067A60` (Sage deep), white text | Main CTA. One per screen. |
| **Secondary** | Transparent, `#067A60` 1.5px border + text | Supporting actions. |
| **Ghost** | Transparent, `#D8E8E2` 1px border, mid-grey text | Tertiary actions, settings, cancel. |

Hover states:

- Primary → background shifts to Sage mid `#0B9A78`.
- Secondary → background fills with Sage tint `#E8F5F0`.
- Ghost → border becomes Sage light `#7ACBB0`, text becomes Sage mid.

Buttons usually pair with a Lucide icon (16px, stroke 2) on the left of the
label — e.g. `play` for *Start ride*, `bar-chart-3` for *View session*,
`settings` for *Settings*, `check` for *Save*.

## Badges and labels

Pill-shaped (`border-radius: 20px`), PT Sans Bold 11px, padding 3px × 10px.

| Variant | Background | Text | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sage | `#D4EDE4` | `#034D3A` | Gait names, neutral categories |
| Purple | `#E8DDF5` | `#4A2080` | Secondary accents only |

Horizontal stacked bar showing time spent in each gait across a session. 10–12px

**light** for badge backgrounds and **dark** for hover/active states.

**Size scale**

- 16px — inline with body text
- 20px — dense UI, table rows
- 24px — buttons, primary actions
- 32px+ — feature illustrations

`bar-chart-3` · `heart` · `clock` · `calendar` · `compass` · `map-pin` ·
