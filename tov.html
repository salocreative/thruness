Voice

Tone of Voice

How we write. What we sound like. The same person, every channel, every time.

Voice in four words
Honest

We tell riders what their data says. We respect them enough to give them the truth.

Quiet

We speak when there is something worth saying. We never shout. We never push.

Knowing

We understand horses. We speak the language of the arena without being exclusive.

Reverent

We treat the bond between horse and rider as sacred. We never reduce it to a metric.

General voice
✓ Write like this
Short sentences. Let them land.
Speak directly to the rider.
Use concrete, specific language.
Trust the rider to understand.
Use the language of equestrian sport naturally.
✕ Not like this
Long clauses that build to the point.
Speak about riders in the third person.
Use vague, motivational filler.
Over-explain everything.
Use jargon to sound knowledgeable.
Words we use and avoid
✓ Use
Ride, session, work
Movement, rhythm, connection
Gait, stride, tempo
Track, record, analyse
Horse, rider — by name where possible
✕ Avoid
Journey, adventure, experience
Amazing, incredible, awesome
Leverage, optimise, synergy
Engage, onboard, action (as a verb)
Pet, animal, buddy
App strings and UI labels

Short, verb-led, no punctuation at end of labels. Buttons are actions. Error messages explain what happened and what to do next.

Writing for the horse
The bond between horse and rider is spiritual. We write with that weight in mind. We never reduce the horse to a data point or the ride to a metric.
✓ Write like this
Bella's trot showed more regularity this session.
She's carrying herself better through the corners.
Something shifted between you both in that last canter.
He gave everything today.
✕ Not like this
Your horse's performance score increased.
The animal's gait data suggests improvement.
Canter metric improved by 12%.
Output optimised.
Quick rules
No exclamation marks

Not in UI, not in push notifications, not in email.

Lead with the information

Don't warm up to the point. Start with it.

No emoji in UI

Emoji in social only, when it adds meaning.

UK English always

Colour, analyse, organise, recognise.

Horse = he / she / they

Never "it". Never "the animal". By name where possible.

Voice prompts under 6 words

Calm. Verb or noun. No questions.
